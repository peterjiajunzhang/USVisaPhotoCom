DS-160 Photo Upload Failed? File, Photo & Session Fixes

By the US Visa Photo developer · Reviewed November 18, 2026

What happened on DS-160?

  • The upload rejected my file
  • The form flagged my photo
  • The session timed out or the service failed
  • My confirmation page shows an X

The upload rejected my file

Check the copy you selected in the upload picker. It should be a JPEG with equal sides of 600–1200 pixels and a file size of 240 kB or less.

  • Convert HEIC to JPEG
  • Crop to a square without stretching
  • Reduce the file size to 240 kB or less

See the official digital image specifications for color and compression requirements.

Need a new JPEG? Preview it in the app

US Visa Photo crops your image, replaces the background with white and shows file size and framing. Preview and retry for free on iPhone or Android. One purchase unlocks one JPEG export at the price shown in the app.

Follow the State Department’s editing rules for your submission. If you need an unedited photo, retake it against a plain white or off-white background. Read more about cropping and editing limits.

The form flagged my photo

Check the original for blur, shadows, a busy background or a turned head. If you need a retake, face the camera straight on in even light against a plain white or off-white background. Use the State Department’s photo examples to check framing and lighting before you prepare the file.

The session timed out or the service failed

When the site is available, return to the official DS-160 application and select Retrieve an Application. Enter your application ID and answer the prompts. The State Department’s DS-160 FAQ says completed pages are saved and gives instructions for returning to the last completed page. You may need to re-enter unsaved information.

My confirmation page shows an X

An X in the photo box on the confirmation page means the photo upload failed. The State Department’s DS-160 FAQ says to provide a printed photo that meets its requirements with the confirmation page and contact the U.S. embassy or consulate handling your application for specific instructions.

What can the app’s checklist tell you?

Review pixels, file size, head height, eye position and lighting warnings before you export.
